# Duckworth

> A collaborative family shopping assistant that turns scattered household needs into one clear, coordinated path to purchase.

## Product Vision

Duckworth makes shared shopping feel effortless. Anyone in a household can add what they need immediately—by speaking, typing, or tapping—without stopping to provide perfect details. Duckworth fills in what it can, remembers the household's usual choices, keeps everyone in sync, and asks for attention only when a decision genuinely requires a person.

The result is more than a shared list. It is a calm coordination layer between noticing a need and buying the right thing from the right place.

## The Problem

Household shopping is a collaborative activity managed with tools built for individual note-taking. Requests arrive through conversation, messages, memory, and hurried list entries. They are often incomplete, duplicated, or changed without everyone seeing the update. Preferences live in people's heads, and important decisions are mixed together with routine ones.

This creates familiar failures:

- Items are forgotten because adding them takes too much effort in the moment.
- Entries such as “milk” or “detergent” lack the detail needed to buy confidently.
- Two people buy the same thing because neither can see the other's intent or progress.
- Routine preferences must be restated on every shopping trip.
- People are interrupted for choices the system could have resolved safely.
- A complete list still leaves the work of deciding where each item should be purchased.

Duckworth addresses the entire coordination problem while keeping the experience as simple as adding an item to a list.

## Who It Serves

Duckworth is for families and other households in which multiple people contribute to, plan, or complete shopping. It should work equally well for a person capturing a need in seconds, a person organizing an upcoming purchase, and a person actively shopping.

No one should need to become the household's list administrator for the system to stay useful.

## The Product Experience

Every item moves through a simple lifecycle:

1. **Capture** — A household member adds a need immediately by voice, text, or a quick tap.
2. **Understand** — Duckworth interprets incomplete language and adds likely details from context and household history.
3. **Coordinate** — The shared state updates for everyone, even when several people contribute at once.
4. **Resolve** — Routine choices happen quietly; ambiguous, conflicting, or consequential choices are surfaced to the right person.
5. **Guide** — When the household is ready to buy, Duckworth helps direct each item toward a suitable purchasing destination.
6. **Learn** — Confirmed choices and completed purchases improve future suggestions without making preferences feel permanent or difficult to change.

## Core Capabilities

| Capability | Product intent |
| --- | --- |
| Frictionless capture | Make adding an item faster than sending a message or relying on memory. |
| Intelligent completion | Turn shorthand and incomplete entries into useful shopping intent without demanding a form. |
| Household memory | Remember recurring products, quantities, variants, and preferences when the household confirms them through use. |
| Duplicate prevention | Detect likely overlap before it becomes an accidental duplicate purchase, while allowing intentional multiples. |
| Live collaboration | Maintain one dependable shared state as household members add, edit, claim, or complete items concurrently. |
| Attention filtering | Handle high-confidence routine work quietly and surface only ambiguity, conflict, risk, or meaningful choice. |
| Purchase guidance | Help each item reach an appropriate destination when purchasing begins, without locking the product to a destination model prematurely. |

## Product Principles

### Capture first, clarify later

The system should never make a person complete a detailed workflow just to record a need. A rough entry saved now is more valuable than a perfect entry forgotten.

### Quiet intelligence

Duckworth should reduce household coordination work, not replace it with notifications and confirmation prompts. Automation earns trust by being useful, visible when relevant, and easy to correct.

### One shared truth

Every household member should be able to understand what is needed, what changed, and what is already being handled. Concurrent activity must not produce silent conflicts or divergent lists.

### Memory with user control

Household history should make common choices easier while remaining editable and reversible. A past preference is helpful context, not an unchangeable rule.

### Prevent mistakes without blocking intent

Duplicate detection and clarification should guide rather than police. Duckworth must distinguish likely mistakes from deliberate quantities, replacements, and separate needs.

### Decisions deserve attention; routine does not

The product should reserve interruptions for moments where human judgment changes the outcome. Everything else should progress with minimal ceremony.

## What Success Looks Like

Duckworth succeeds when:

- Household members capture needs as soon as they notice them.
- Brief entries become specific enough to act on with little or no follow-up.
- Everyone trusts that the shared list reflects current intent.
- Accidental duplicate purchases become uncommon.
- Routine purchases require less repeated explanation over time.
- Questions are infrequent, timely, and clearly worth answering.
- Moving from a settled list to purchasing feels guided rather than manually reorganized.

The north-star outcome is simple: the household spends less attention coordinating shopping while becoming more confident that the right things will be bought.

## Deferred Decisions

This overview intentionally does not define:

- Whether purchasing guidance initially prioritizes physical stores, online retailers, or a combination.
- Which retailers, commerce services, assistants, or household platforms Duckworth integrates with.
- The ranking model used to select a purchasing destination.
- Detailed interaction design for voice, mobile, web, notifications, or in-store use.
- Technical architecture, synchronization strategy, or intelligence providers.
- Monetization, partnerships, geographic rollout, and retailer-specific constraints.

These decisions should be made through focused product discovery and technical design after the north-star experience is validated.
